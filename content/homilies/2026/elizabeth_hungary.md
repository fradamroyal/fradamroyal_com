Zacchaeus finds salvation. Notice the order in which things happen in his life. First, he hears about Jesus in some way. The Gospel doesn't tell us exactly how, but what he hears intrigues him. And so he wants to go out and see Jesus.

He does everything he has to do to make that happen, and he sees the Lord. In that moment, Jesus offers him an invitation: he invites himself over to Zacchaeus's house. Zacchaeus says yes, and that is the moment when he is transformed. In saying yes to Jesus' invitation, he repents of the evil he has done. He is going to repay anyone he has wronged four times over, and he is going to give to the poor. Zacchaeus finds salvation by first hearing about Jesus, then seeing him, and then accepting his invitation. And his life is changed.

I think that same process works itself out in people's lives today. It begins with hearing about Jesus, and that isn't so difficult in this world. People hear about him in conversations, in podcasts, and through all sorts of media. They hear about him, and they are intrigued by who he is.

But then comes the more challenging part: they have to see him. If someone wanted to see Jesus today, where would we send them? We could send them in here, because he is there in the tabernacle, waiting for us day after day. But unless we already have faith, we will not recognize that he is there.

Think about the Jesus Zacchaeus hears about. He hears about Jesus going out and preaching, serving people, healing, and offering forgiveness. Those are the things that intrigue him. If someone came here without faith and looked toward the tabernacle, they would not see that service to others. They would not see that forgiveness. They would not see all those things that intrigued Zacchaeus.

So where would we send them? The place to see Jesus in the world today should be in us, in our actions. We are the ones who should be out there preaching. We are the ones who should be healing and serving, doing all those things Jesus did in his earthly ministry. Christ continues to be present to this world in his Body.

If people cannot see Christ within us and in our actions in the world, then they will not see him. And if they do not see him, they will not find that invitation, and they will not find salvation.

So it is imperative that each of us go out and do the works of Jesus. That is fundamental to the Christian life. The Christian life is not meant to be lived at home in isolation, without any interaction with the world. We are meant to go out and proclaim who Christ is.

Some of us will be better at public proclamation, at defending the faith and spreading the word. Others will be better at serving, at helping the poor and those in need. Do whatever God has called you to do, but we must go out, because that is where people truly encounter Christ.

And yes, once they have the eyes of faith, they can come here. They can continue to grow and develop. But they must first see him in our actions and in our choices. That is the only way to truly find that encounter and ultimately hear Christ's invitation to that wonderful gift of life.
