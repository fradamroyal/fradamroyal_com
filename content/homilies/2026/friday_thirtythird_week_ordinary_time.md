We have to remember that this scene in the Gospel immediately follows the reading we heard yesterday. Jesus was looking out over Jerusalem, sad at what was going to happen to the city because they did not recognize the time of their visitation. He had offered them a path to peace, and they did not choose it.

And immediately after that moment of mourning over Jerusalem, Jesus goes into the temple and begins driving people out. He tells them that they’ve made his house a den of thieves when it should have been a house of prayer. Christ seems to be responding to what he sees in Jerusalem, and I think the connection between those two scenes tells us where Jerusalem went wrong.

Their corruption, their inability to hear the message Christ was preaching to them, began in the temple, in their worship itself. They stopped worshiping God as they had been taught. They stopped praying as they were called to. And from there, they lost the ability to hear what God wanted to say to them. In the end, Jerusalem was destroyed.

And that’s important for us to recognize because it tells us something about our own lives. When corruption seeps in, it begins in the spiritual life itself. The world cannot make us stop praying. The world cannot make us stop being Christian. It is our own choices in the spiritual life that will ultimately lead us there.

We choose whether or not we pray. The world can’t force us to stop. We can pray silently our head at any time without anyone noticing. We choose whether we participate in worship. We choose whether to come to mass. These are our choices to make.

If we stop praying, if we stop coming to mass, if we stop doing the things we know develop our spiritual life and draw us closer to the Lord, then the world can begin flooding in. We make room for it, and we lose the ability to hear what God wants to say to us.

Therefore our priority has to be the spiritual life. Too often we get that wrong. I am often asked the question, “Father, how do I fit in all of my prayers?” That’s the wrong question. The question is, “How do I fit in the rest of my life, given the prayers I want to say?” Yes, we have to choose our spiritual practices according to our own needs and our state in life. But we have to give those practices priority. We make time for prayer. We make time to come to mass. We make time to truly encounter Christ in our lives. And the rest of life will fit in where it needs to.

If we can hear God’s voice and respond to whatever he is calling us to in this moment, he will see that everything else is taken care of. God doesn’t want most of us to run off to the desert and pray twenty-four hours a day. He wants us to live right where he has called us, to take care of our families, our friends, and our neighbors in need. That is what he wants us doing. When we enter into prayer as we should, it leads us to do those very things. And as we see here, we need prayer to find the path he truly wants us on.

So we must value prayer and the spiritual life above all else. We have to plan for them and make time for them, and then allow God to do the rest. He will not abandon us. He will make time for everything else. We must listen to him and he will show us the path that leads to peace.
