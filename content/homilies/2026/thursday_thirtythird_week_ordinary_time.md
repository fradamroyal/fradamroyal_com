I find this to be one of the saddest scenes in the Gospel. You can feel the Lord’s own sadness as he looks out over Jerusalem, his city. The story of the Old Testament has been building toward this city, toward God’s temple standing there and his people living out their relationship with him there.

And now he tells them that all of it is going to be destroyed. We know what came in the year seventy, when the Romans sacked Jerusalem and destroyed the temple. Jesus came for our salvation, and here we see what he came to offer the people of Jerusalem. They had a choice. They could have taken the way of peace he taught them and avoided that destruction. They turned away from what he offered.

That’s hard to hear. I’m sure it was hard for him to say, seeing everything that was coming. And we need to hear it, because we have a choice before us too. Will we follow the way of peace that Christ offers us, or will we follow the way of the world?

We know where the way of the world leads. We’ve seen it throughout human history. It leads to more violence, more destruction, and more depravity. And still we struggle to believe that the way of peace really does lead to peace. When we look at the world, it’s hard to believe that turning the other cheek is a good idea. How could loving my enemies and praying for those who persecute me make a difference?

We look at the way of Christ and think, “If I do those things, the world is going to mistreat me. Everything is going to fall apart. I need to do what the world says.” And yet all of human history tells us that the world’s way doesn’t work.

No nation on earth has ever written the Gospel into its laws and truly tried to live by it. Individuals have. Some of the saints have made great progress in imitating Christ. And Christ is calling all people to follow the way of peace. When you make the effort to love your enemy, to be holy, to imitate Christ in all of your life, God’s protection comes with it. God himself is with you, and he is the one you want with you.

You can see Israel struggle with this throughout its history. God tells his people what to do. They follow him for a time, and he gives them the peace he promised. Then they go their own way and lose it all again. God reaches out and offers it again, and things go well for them for a time while they obey him and try to be holy. He fulfills his promises. And then they go their own way again. We do the same thing in our own lives, and we do it as nations.

As Christians, we have to follow the way God lays out for us, even when we’re afraid it will make our lives worse. We have to trust that God will keep his promises and that he will be with us if we do what he commands. Even when we reject him, he is there, ready to offer us mercy. We miss out on the peace he wants to give us when we keep turning away from the things that make that peace possible.

I hope all of us can sit down and reflect on the choices we’ve made and on the choices humanity has made throughout history. How many times do we have to try the world’s way before we see that it doesn’t work? Living out the Gospel makes a difference, and the more people who do it, the greater the difference it makes. Peace really is possible, and it comes through imitating Christ.

So we need to change how we live and how we preach this message. Some of the things Christ asks of us seem difficult and counterintuitive. They are the only way to peace, and we have yet to try living them out together. When we make those choices, we let God take charge through the things we actually do. It is only with him that we will find the peace we are looking for.

Every day, in every moment, we make that choice. Do I want what the world offers, what seems to make sense even though I know it will never work? Or do I want what Christ offers? It may be hard to choose what Christ offers in this moment. It’s the one thing that may actually bring about what I hope for.
