A straightforward reading of this Gospel would tell us that the Lord is talking about the destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple. But many people read it at a symbolic level and take it to be talking about the end of time as well. I have no objection to that. I think it fits within the tradition.

But even if it is talking about the end of time, we’re still left with the question of what we’re supposed to do with the Lord’s words. Some people would hear this and think he’s giving us a kind of map of history, as though we could figure out exactly when the world will end just by looking for the earthquakes and famines and plagues and wars and all of that. But I don’t think that’s what he intends to tell us. It’s far too broad to give us that kind of direction.

Instead, I think the Lord is reminding his disciples that God is still in control. He is the God of all history, in our future just as much as in our present and in our past. He has already foreseen and permitted everything that’s going to happen, and he will prepare his people for that time.

The disciples weren’t meant to walk away thinking they now had to figure out exactly when it was going to happen and prepare themselves. They were supposed to walk away reassured that even when all these terrible things happen, they have nothing to worry about. A Christian fundamentally never has to worry because God is always in control. God is always with his people, and he is always going to protect them. He will bring about whatever is good for us in those moments.

And it’s important that we hear that, because when we get into those difficult situations, when it feels like the entire world is collapsing, we naturally struggle. We worry and think, “What should I do?”

And here the Lord is telling us, just keep living your life. Just keep trying to follow him. Keep praying. Keep trying to be holy. Keep going out and serving the poor. Keep doing all those things you would normally do, because God is still in control.

And I think we have that tendency to worry, especially today, because we have news around the clock. We can watch what’s happening anywhere in the world at any time. We can always see some disaster somewhere and worry about it. And right now it feels like there are a lot of disasters in a lot of places. But whether it’s the end or not really doesn’t matter, because God is still in control.

We have nothing to worry about. We have nothing to prepare for in that sense. We are called to continue doing what we’ve always done throughout history. To pray. To be holy. To be of service. To grow in charity. That is what our focus should be. We are called to imitate and live the life of Jesus Christ. That is the Christian’s calling, and it is where our focus should always be.

The world will do what the world is going to do. But this is not our final home. This is our pilgrim journey. As the Church says, we are on our way home. And so that is where our hearts should be, looking toward eternity and preparing for that moment. The life we are preparing for will last forever and can never be taken away from us.
