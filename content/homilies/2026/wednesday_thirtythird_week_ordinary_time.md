Luke’s version of the parable we heard this past Sunday gives the whole situation a different flavor. I think Luke is telling us something about why the Lord delays his return. The Lord could bring his kingdom to completion at once. He gives us time, and in that time he reveals our own character to us.

Look at what happens in the parable. The master entrusts money to his servants. Some of them see right away what they’re supposed to do with it. They go to work and bring back more than they received. The master gives them a chance to put their righteousness into practice, and everyone can see it in what they do.

One servant simply hides the money. His own actions should tell him something. He is lazy, he is ungrateful, and he really does not love his master. When he comes back, he has an excuse. He says he knew his master was harsh and wanted to make sure he lost nothing. The master calls him a wicked servant and judges him by his own words. He sees through the excuse. The servant’s actions have already shown his laziness, and now he suffers the consequences.

Then there are the people who never wanted this man as their king. That part is unique to Luke’s version. The king orders them brought before him and slain. This is one of those moments when Christ reminds us how much our choices matter, even in the face of all his mercy and all his love.

By the end of the parable, everyone’s character has come to light. The righteous have shown their righteousness in what they did, and they inherit the reward prepared for them, cities in this case. The lazy servant refuses to face what his actions have shown him and loses even what he had. And those who openly rebelled meet their punishment.

God’s delay in returning and his mercy both serve a purpose. He gives us time to become righteous and to live that out in our actions. In that same time, our actions can reveal that we simply do not want what Christ offers us.

That is the choice before us in every moment of our lives. Do we really want what Christ has to offer us? If we do, we have to act on it. We have to become who he has told us to be. Laziness and open rebellion can take us to the same end, a complete rejection of him. The choice before us is whether we will follow him with our whole mind, heart, and soul.

We have to make that choice again and again throughout the day. It is so easy to fall off the path and barely notice. Like the lazy servant, we can think we’re doing all right and find an excuse for ourselves. Before the Lord, all our excuses fall away. He reveals the truth of who we are.

So let us call this to mind every day and remember the purpose of our lives. Let any fear of punishment give way to love for our master, love for the Lord. He wants good things for us, and receiving them comes at a cost. That cost is our entire life.
