I commonly hear this Gospel passage cited in a context it doesn’t apply to. People use it to say we never have to prepare for anything at all, that preparation of any sort is actually contrary to the will of God. If we’re unprepared, the Spirit will speak through us. If we’re prepared, the Spirit won’t speak through us. That’s how people use the passage. And that’s a confusion of spontaneity and randomness with the Holy Spirit.

We get this idea that if our humanity is at all present, if we’re using our reason, then somehow the Spirit is shut out. But God created our humanity. He created our nature. He gave us our intellect and the gift of reason. In fact, all of those things are a share in God’s own nature. God is reasonable, and that’s what makes us able to be reasonable. And so the Holy Spirit is present when we use those gifts. In fact, the Holy Spirit can guide us in using them.

In normal circumstances you should be using your reason. You should be preparing, doing all the things you’d expect to do, and letting the Holy Spirit guide you in all of it. But we don’t depend entirely on ourselves. We recognize our own failings and fallibility. Yet we still have to engage the whole of our human nature in what we do. That’s what Christ calls us to do.

Here Christ is giving a particular promise to a particular people. He’s speaking to his first disciples, at a time when they could still be hauled in front of a synagogue. Today, if you’re persecuted for being a Christian, nobody’s hauling you into the synagogue. Maybe a prison, but definitely not the synagogue.

But I think at the heart of his message is this: don’t worry. Don't worry about your preparations. Don’t worry about what you will say. In the passages leading up to this, he’s talking about what the future is going to look like and all the horrible things that are going to come. And the whole time he’s telling his disciples, don’t worry. God is with you.

And here the Lectionary leaves out the opening words of the verse. Jesus says, “Before all this happens, however,” and then he tells them they’re going to be arrested and thrown into prison. So I think he’s continuing the same thought and telling them again, don’t worry. Go out and live out the mission. Do the things you have to do to convert people. And don’t spend your time thinking about what you’re going to say when they persecute you, when they haul you in front of judges and political leaders. Don’t worry about preparing that speech. Just stay focused on the mission.

And that’s true in our own lives. It would be easy to waste your life preparing for the worst possible outcomes instead of living it. Of course we have to prepare. But we can’t live every moment worrying and trying to get ready for all the bad things that could happen. Most of them probably won’t. And even if they do, we still won’t be ready for all of them. We have to trust that God is in control, that he’s the God of all of history, and that his providence will take care of us in the way we truly need to be cared for.

Because this is not our home. Our home is in eternal life with him, and that’s where he’s going to take us.

And so, as Christians, we must not worry. We must allow the Holy Spirit to keep doing his work. We must keep our minds and hearts focused on the mission of the Gospel, spreading the good news and growing in holiness ourselves. If we do that, if we stay focused on that, we will surely end up exactly where we want to be.
