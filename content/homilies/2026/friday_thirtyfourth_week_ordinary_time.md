After disasters have hit an area, we have to begin the process of rebuilding. And people don’t always rebuild things exactly as they were before. Sometimes they learned something in the midst of all the chaos. Sometimes, having lived in an area for a time, they just want some change now. They’ve always thought, “I wish this were a little different.” And now they have an opportunity.

But you also see that people themselves change. The process of rebuilding is introspective. They spend time reflecting on their lives and on what it means to have lived through all the things they’ve seen. And so they, too, end up being rebuilt a little differently. They’ve faced their mortality in some way, or they’ve realized that the things of this world don’t last, and they decide to live differently. And so everything can end up being very different, often for the better, if people really take the time to reflect.

I think that is what the Lord is talking about. When we hear his words, partly because of our culture and the people we live around, we may think he’s talking about the end of time. I think he’s speaking of something much nearer. He’s telling them that these things will happen soon, and that when they see them happen, they’ll know the kingdom of God is near.

That’s the same message he was preaching when he went into every town and district and region saying, “The kingdom of God is near.” When he sent out the disciples, he told them to say, “The kingdom of God is at hand for you.” The Gospel is being preached to you. The good news has come to you. God is here to help you. He uses those words to tell you that now is the time to repent.

And so I think he’s speaking about a coming disaster, which we would recognize in the fall of the Temple. He’s telling them that this will be an opportunity to reflect, to change their lives and convert. And that is, in fact, what we see in the early Church. The fall of the Temple came after Nero’s persecutions. After these upheavals, the Church began to find a little freedom and to grow rapidly because the kingdom of God was near.

And that is the process of conversion for us as Christians. We recognize that the kingdom of God is near, and we choose to live in the light of that. We change our lives. Sometimes, for some people, that takes a disaster. It takes having everything taken away before they realize, “I need to do things differently. I need to change my life.” But we don’t have to wait for that moment.

Every day is a good day to be converted. Every day is a good day to reflect and think about what our lives should really look like. We’ve already heard the message, and we know it’s true. The kingdom of God is always near to us. We should take that to heart now, before disaster or tragedy strikes, and keep converting and proclaiming that same message to others. That was the message Jesus gave to everyone he sent out. “The kingdom of God is near.” And that should be our message as well.

Yes, human history will come to an end one day. We have no control over that, and we have no idea when it will be. But we do know there will be local disasters. There will be tragedies, and people will have to rebuild. We should be examples to them of what it means to live a reflective life and to build our lives on the solid foundation that is Christ himself. We should show them what it means to believe that the kingdom of God is near, because it is. It is for each of us individually. It is for the world.

Christ has come into the world to bring us light. That is what we will begin celebrating here at Advent. His coming has changed human history. And we must live in the light of that, recognizing that his kingdom is near to us and that it opens up to all of us the possibility of eternal life and happiness in his presence forever.
